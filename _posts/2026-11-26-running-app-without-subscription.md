It's Black Friday week, and every running app has a deal. Before you take one, it's worth knowing what you'd get without paying at all. For a lot of runners the honest answer is "most of what you need", and the subscription only makes sense for one specific thing: a structured plan, a route planner, or a training log that goes back years.

Here's what's free and what's paid in the apps people most often compare, as of late November 2026, including our own. We've tried to be fair; where iRunning's free tier is thinner than someone else's, we say so.

## The short version

| App | Price (US) | Free trial | What's free | Account needed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nike Run Club** | Free | — | Run tracking, guided runs, training plans | Yes, a Nike Member account |
| **Strava** | $11.99/month or $79.99/year | 30 days | Recording, the social feed, segment top 10, Beacon from your phone | Yes |
| **Runna** | $19.99/month or $119.99/year | 7 days (longer through Strava) | The first week of any plan, run recording | Yes |
| **Strava + Runna** | $149.99/year | 30 days for new Strava users | — | Yes |
| **Garmin Connect+** | $6.99/month or $69.99/year | 30 days | Garmin Coach plans, activity details, health data, basic LiveTrack | Yes, and a Garmin device |
| **Apple Workout app** | Free with Apple Watch | — | Custom interval workouts, pace and heart-rate alerts, Race Route, Pacer | Your Apple Account |
| **iRunning** | Free, or Premium | Not needed: the free version has no time limit | Watch app, intervals, heart-rate zones, voice cues, one plan, share cards and videos | **No** |

Prices are US list prices from each app's own page or the App Store in late October 2026; local prices differ, and Black Friday offers change them for a week or two. In the UK, Runna is £15.99 a month or £99.99 a year.

## Nike Run Club: free, with an account

Nike Run Club is the obvious answer to "free running app". Run tracking, hundreds of audio-guided runs and full training plans from a first 5K to a marathon are free, with no premium tier. The catch is the account: you sign in with a Nike Member profile, which is free but feeds Nike's marketing.

**Good for:** guided runs, coaching voices, running without paying anything.
**Less good for:** custom interval workouts, heart-rate-zone training, and some guided runs are only available in some countries.

## Strava: free to record, paid to plan

Strava's free tier records your runs, puts them in your friends' feeds and shows the top 10 on segments. You can share your live location with Beacon from the phone app. The subscription adds the things that make it a training tool: route planning and offline maps, a training log, goals, full segment leaderboards, fitness and freshness charts, and Beacon from a watch.

**Good for:** the social side, which is free and is why most people use it.
**Less good for:** structured training. Strava doesn't build workouts or plans on its own; that's why it bought Runna.

## Runna: plans first, and plans are paid

Runna is a coaching app: personalised plans for 5K to marathon with paces worked out for you. Without a subscription you get the first week of any plan; after that, it's paid. Since June 2026 that includes the beginner plans (New to Running, Return to Running), which used to be free. It needs an account, and the Strava bundle saves about $50 a year if you'd pay for both anyway.

**Good for:** runners who want a plan that adjusts paces to them and are happy to pay for it.
**Less good for:** anyone looking for a free plan, or for a running app that works without an account.

## Garmin and Apple: the free tools on your wrist

If you already own a Garmin or an Apple Watch, you've paid for a good free running app with it.

- **Garmin** keeps Garmin Coach plans, activity details and course creation free; Connect+ adds AI insights, a performance dashboard and extra mapping for $6.99 a month.
- **Apple Watch** has interval workouts built into the Workout app since watchOS 9: warm-up, repeats of work and recovery, cool-down, pace and heart-rate alerts, plus Pacer and Race Route. With iOS 26 you can build them on the iPhone too. Apple Fitness+ ($9.99 a month) adds video and audio workouts, but you don't need it for running.

Our guide to [interval running on Apple Watch]({% post_url 2026-11-17-interval-running-apple-watch %}) shows how far the built-in tools go.

## iRunning: no account, and what's free

iRunning doesn't have accounts. There's no sign-up, no email and no login: your runs are stored on your iPhone and in Apple Health, and that's where they stay. Here's the split.

**Free, for as long as you like:**

- **The Apple Watch app.** Run any workout from your wrist, with or without your phone, with haptic taps at every change.
- **Interval workouts with pace and heart-rate targets**, and two custom workouts of your own in the builder, plus the Free run, Tabata and Easy run presets.
- **Heart-rate zones and alerts**, live and as time-in-zone after the run, with an Apple Watch or a Bluetooth chest strap.
- **Voice cues** for every interval, pace and heart-rate alerts, and announcements every kilometre or mile.
- **Add to Apple Watch**: send any workout to Apple's own Workout app.
- **One training plan**, From Walk to Run: six weeks from walking to five-minute runs.
- **Share cards and run videos**: photo overlays and animated route videos for Instagram and TikTok.
- **Imports** from Apple Health, GPX and FIT files, so your history from other apps comes with you.
- **Dark mode, indoor mode, Live Activity** on the lock screen.

**Premium:**

- **All 41 training plans**, from 0→5K to a first 50K, including the Sub-4 Marathon and the walking plans.
- **All 87 preset workouts** and unlimited custom workouts, plus the AI workout builder on iPhones with Apple Intelligence.
- **Your whole history in the app.** Free shows your last seven days; Premium opens every run. Your runs are saved to Apple Health either way.
- **Export**: GPX and FIT files, batch export and sending runs to Strava.
- **More run screens and Watch faces**, and iCloud sync.

The honest comparison: if you want free *plans*, Nike Run Club gives you more of them than we do. If you want free *structure* (interval workouts with targets, zones, voice cues and a real Watch app) without creating an account anywhere, that's where iRunning's free tier is strongest.

## How to decide

- **You mainly want to record runs and see them with friends:** Strava free, or Nike Run Club.
- **You want someone to tell you what to run, and you'll pay for it:** Runna, or iRunning Premium if you'd rather not have an account, or a plan in Garmin Coach if you have a Garmin.
- **You want free intervals with targets on an Apple Watch:** the Workout app, or iRunning free.
- **You want to start running from nothing, for free:** Nike Run Club's beginner plan, the NHS Couch to 5K app in the UK, or iRunning's From Walk to Run.

You don't have to pick only one. Plenty of runners use a planning app for workouts and Strava for the social feed, and both read from and write to Apple Health. Our guide to [moving your runs between apps]({% post_url 2026-09-05-import-runs-from-nike-strava-garmin %}) covers how.

## A word on trials

Most trials turn into a yearly subscription unless you cancel. If you start one this week, set a reminder for the day before it ends. On iPhone, you can see and cancel every App Store subscription under **Settings › your name › Subscriptions**.
