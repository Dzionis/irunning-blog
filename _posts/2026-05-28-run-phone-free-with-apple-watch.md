There's a particular freedom in stepping out the door with nothing but a watch. No armband, no bouncing pocket, no phone to juggle at the water fountain. If you own an Apple Watch, iRunning is built to make that your default.

## Build on the phone, run from the wrist

The big screen is for planning; the small one is for running. You build and edit workouts on your iPhone — intervals, repeat blocks, targets — and your library syncs to the Watch automatically. On the run, the Watch is all you need:

- Start any saved workout straight from your wrist
- See the current interval, time or distance remaining, and your pace at a glance
- Heart rate comes straight from the Watch's sensor — no chest strap required

The run screen follows the same rule on the Watch as on the phone: one big number, nothing you have to squint at. Mid-interval, you only ever need one piece of information — how much is left — and it's the thing filling the screen.

## Haptics: the tap on the wrist

Here's the feature Watch runners end up loving most: you don't have to look at the screen at all.

Every interval change comes with a distinct haptic tap. Work starts — tap. Recovery starts — different tap. After a couple of runs you stop checking the screen entirely, because your wrist already told you what to do. Combined with voice cues in your headphones, you can run an entire eight-interval session without glancing down once.

## Truly standalone

The Watch app doesn't need the phone nearby. Leave your iPhone at home and the Watch records the whole thing: intervals, heart rate, and the workout itself. When you're back in range, the run syncs to your phone — full stats, splits, and charts waiting for you.

Everything counts toward your Activity rings too, because runs are saved as real workouts in Apple Health.

## Why this matters for intervals specifically

Plenty of apps track a steady run from a watch. Intervals are harder: the workout has structure, and the watch has to carry that structure with it — what interval you're in, what's next, when to alert you.

That's the part iRunning was built around. The Watch isn't a remote control for the phone; it runs the session itself. Pause on the wrist, skip an interval on the wrist, end the run on the wrist.

## Getting set up

1. Install iRunning on your iPhone and open it once
2. The Watch app installs automatically (or grab it from the Watch's App Store)
3. Build a workout on the phone — it appears on the Watch
4. Tap it and run

If you've been waiting for a reason to leave the phone at home, this is it.
