The best photo from your run is rarely a map. It's the fog on the lake at kilometre three, the view from the top of the hill, or the post-run grin on a bridge.

iRunning's **transparent overlays** let you keep that photo and still show the numbers. Each overlay is just your stats and route on a see-through background, so your photo shows through everywhere else. Paste it on top in Instagram Stories and you're done.

## How to do it

1. **Open the run.** Go to **History**, tap your run, then tap **Share**. If you're asked where to share, choose **Image**.
2. **Pick an overlay.** Keep the format on **Story · 9:16**, tap the **Transparent** filter, and swipe through the designs. The preview shows a checkerboard wherever the overlay is see-through.
3. **Tap Copy.** The overlay goes to your clipboard as a transparent PNG at full story resolution, 1080 × 1920.
4. **Paste it on your photo.** In Instagram, start a new story with your photo as the background, then paste the copied overlay as a sticker. Pinch to resize and drag it wherever the photo has room.

**Other ways to use them:**

- **Save** puts the overlay in Photos, handy if you're editing later.
- **Share** (top right) sends the PNG file to any app, such as Canva, CapCut or a messaging app. Sending the file keeps the transparent background intact.

Transparent overlays are story-format only, and every one of them is **free**.

## 17 designs, and when to use each

Not every overlay suits every run. Some only appear when the run has the data they need, so a run without GPS won't offer the route designs, and a run without a heart-rate source won't offer the heart-rate ones.

**Simple and clean: for any run**

| Overlay | What it shows | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Centre stack** | Distance, pace and time stacked, with the route below | Photos with a calm centre, like sky or water |
| **Ledger** | The three stats flush left with thin dividers | Busy photos. It hugs one edge |
| **Classic row** | Distance, pace and time side by side | Keeping the photo the star |
| **Five stats** | Adds elevation gain and calories | When the climb is the story |
| **Hero number** | Distance huge, pace, time and heart rate underneath | A milestone: first 10K, longest run |
| **Distance hero** | Big distance, pace and time, route below | Same idea, a little calmer |
| **Edge rail** | The stats rotated up the left edge | Portraits where the middle is taken |
| **Terminal log** | Monospace readout: distance, pace, moving and elapsed time, heart rate | Runners who think in data |

**Route-led: needs GPS**

| Overlay | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Route first** | Your route large, stats as a caption |
| **Side by side** | Route on the left, stats on the right |

**Heart rate: needs an Apple Watch or a chest strap**

| Overlay | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **HR route** | Your route coloured by heart-rate zone, with average and max heart rate |
| **HR trace** | Zone-coloured route plus a heart-rate sparkline |
| **Zone bars** | Time in each zone as bars |
| **Zone split** | Time in zones, hottest first, with a grid of stats |
| **HR ticker** | A thin zone route with a one-line heart-rate summary |

**Interval workouts: needs a structured session**

| Overlay | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Rep ladder** | A bar for every rep and recovery, with its pace, like *8 × 400m* |
| **Workout card** | Workout name, reps completed and your best rep |

Walks get the same designs, with steps in place of pace where it makes sense.

## Tips for photos that work

- **Leave space when you take the photo.** Frame with sky, water, road or grass above or below your subject. That's where the numbers go.
- **Dark backgrounds win.** Overlays use white type with a soft shadow, so they read best on dusk skies, shaded trails, night streets and deep water. Bright snow, a white wall or a hazy midday sky will wash them out; if that's the shot you have, pick a spot in the photo that's darker, or turn the photo's exposure down a touch first.
- **Match the overlay to the run.** Rep ladder for interval sessions, HR route when heart rate is the point, Hero number for a distance you're proud of.
- **Resize, don't crowd.** Pinch the overlay down to leave the photo breathing room. It's a full-resolution PNG, so it stays sharp at any size.
