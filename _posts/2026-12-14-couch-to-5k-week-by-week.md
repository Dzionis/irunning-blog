Couch to 5K is the most successful beginner running programme there is. Its idea is simple: three sessions a week of walking and running, the running bits getting a little longer each time, until nine weeks later you run 5K, or 30 minutes, without stopping.

Most people who start it don't finish it, though, and they mostly drop out at the same place. Here's what each week of a typical Couch to 5K plan is doing, why week 5 is the wall, and how to get over it.

## The nine weeks at a glance

Every session starts with a 5-minute brisk walk and ends with a 5-minute walk. In between:

| Week | Session 1 | Session 2 | Session 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 × (run 1:00, walk 1:30) | same | 7 × (run 1:00, walk 2:00) |
| 2 | 6 × (run 1:30, walk 2:00) | same | 5 × (run 2:00, walk 2:00) |
| 3 | 5 × (run 3:00, walk 1:30) | same | 4 × (run 4:00, walk 2:00) |
| 4 | 4 × (run 5:00, walk 1:30) | same | 3 × (run 6:00, walk 2:00) |
| 5 | 3 × (run 8:00, walk 2:00) | 2 × (run 10:00, walk 2:00) | **Run 15 minutes nonstop** |
| 6 | 2 × (run 12:00, walk 2:00) | Run 15, walk 3, run 8 | **Run 20 minutes nonstop** |
| 7 | Run 22 min | Run 22 min | Run 25 min |
| 8 | Run 25 min | Run 26 min | Run 28 min |
| 9 | Run 28 min | Run 30 min | **Run 5K** |

This is the version in the iRunning **0 → 5K** plan. The NHS original and other apps differ in the details, but the shape is the same everywhere: short intervals, then longer ones, then the jump to continuous running.

## What each phase is doing

### Weeks 1–3: teaching your body to run at all

Week 1 is eight minutes of running in a half-hour session. It sounds trivial, and that's the point. Your heart and lungs adapt in days, but your tendons, bones and joints take weeks. The early weeks are short so that the structural parts of you can catch up with the engine.

**The most common mistake:** running the one-minute bits too fast. You should be able to talk while you run. If you finish a running interval out of breath, slow down until you're barely faster than your walk. Slow running is still running.

### Week 4: the first long one

Five minutes of running in a row. The first time is a milestone: it's when people start saying "I'm a runner" a little self-consciously.

### Week 5: the wall

Look at the table again. Week 5 starts with eight-minute runs, then ten, then **15 minutes without a walk break**. In most versions of Couch to 5K, week 5 is the biggest jump of the plan, and it's where the drop-out rate peaks.

It's hard for a good reason. Until now, every hard bit had a walk coming. Fifteen minutes is the first run where your brain can't count down to the break. Physically, most people finishing week 4 can do it. Mentally, it feels like a cliff.

How to get over it:

1. **Slow right down.** Run the 15 minutes at the slowest pace that still counts as running. Speed doesn't matter in week 5; finishing does.
2. **Pick a flat route.** Save the hill for week 7.
3. **Split it in your head.** Three blocks of five minutes, which you've done before. Or a podcast episode, or four songs.
4. **If you have to stop, walk for a minute and carry on.** It's still a good session. Repeat it two days later.
5. **Don't skip ahead to week 6 if week 5 didn't work.** Repeat the week. There's no deadline.

### Week 6: 20 minutes

Twenty minutes nonstop by the end of the week. Once you've done it, the rest of the plan is mostly a question of patience.

### Weeks 7–9: from 20 minutes to 5K

All continuous running now, growing a couple of minutes at a time to 30 minutes. The last session is the 5K itself. For most beginners at an easy pace, 5K takes 30–40 minutes. If it takes longer, that's fine: the goal is to run it without stopping, not to run it fast.

## When a week doesn't work

You will have a bad session at some point: a cold, a late night, a week of rain. Three rules:

- **Repeat the week, don't push through it.** Doing week 5 twice is completely normal. The plan is nine weeks long for the average person, not for everyone.
- **Missed a week? Go back one.** If you missed two weeks or more, go back two.
- **Never do two sessions on consecutive days.** The rest day is when your legs adapt. Skipping it is the fastest way to sore shins.

## Why start in December?

Couch to 5K searches peak twice a year: in January and in June. January starters face the darkest, coldest weeks of the year for the first, hardest part of the plan, and they start at the same time as everyone else's resolutions fade.

Start this week and the holidays fall in weeks 2 and 3, when sessions are short and easy to fit in. You hit week 5 in mid-January, with momentum, and you finish in mid-February, just in time for a spring 5K or a parkrun. ## Before Couch to 5K: can you walk for 30 minutes?

If walking briskly for 30 minutes is hard today, start a step earlier. The free **From Walk to Run** plan in iRunning builds up walking first and adds short runs gradually over six weeks. When you finish it, you can skip the first weeks of 0 → 5K.

## After Couch to 5K

Once you can run 30 minutes, you have options:

- **Get faster at 5K.** Our [sub-30 5K guide]({% post_url 2026-09-12-sub-30-5k-training-plan %}) is the natural next goal for most new runners.
- **Go further.** A 10K is eight weeks away; the [first 10K plan]({% post_url 2026-11-27-first-10k-training-plan-8-weeks %}) starts exactly where Couch to 5K finishes.
- **Just keep running.** Three easy runs a week is enough to stay fit for life.
