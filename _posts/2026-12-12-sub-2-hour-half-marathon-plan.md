The two-hour half marathon is the most popular time goal in running after the four-hour marathon, and they're the same pace: **5:41 per kilometre**, or 9:09 per mile. Hold it for 21.1 km and you finish in 1:59:59.

It's a goal that sits right on the edge for a lot of runners. Plenty of people run 2:05 or 2:10 on fitness alone. Getting under two hours usually takes a block of training built around that one pace. Here's what twelve weeks of it look like, and when to start for a spring half.

## Is sub-2 the right goal?

Twelve weeks can take 5–10 minutes off a half marathon. It can't take 25. The plan suits you if:

- **Your last half was between about 2:02 and 2:12**, or your 10K is under about 56 minutes.
- **You run at least three times a week already**, and can make it four.
- **You can do a long run of an hour or more today.** The plan starts at 70 minutes.

If your half is slower than about 2:15, run a regular half-marathon block first. Our [12-week half marathon plan]({% post_url 2026-09-18-half-marathon-training-plan-12-weeks %}) is built for finishing strong, and sub-2 is a great goal for the one after.

## The paces

| Pace | Per km | Per mile | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Half-marathon pace** | 5:36–5:46 | 9:01–9:17 | Tempo blocks and race day |
| **Kilometre reps** | 5:20–5:30 | 8:35–8:51 | 5–6 × 1 km |
| **2 km reps** | 5:30–5:40 | 8:51–9:07 | 3 × 2 km |
| **Long-run finish** | 5:38–5:50 | 9:04–9:23 | The last 20–30 minutes of the long run from week 7 |

Everything is within 20 seconds of goal pace. That's deliberate. The point of the plan isn't to make you fast in general; it's to make 5:41 feel normal.

**Easy runs have no pace at all.** They're run in heart rate zone 2, at a pace where you could chat, which for most sub-2 runners is somewhere around 6:30–7:00 per kilometre. If that sounds slow, our [easy pace chart]({% post_url 2026-12-05-easy-run-pace-chart %}) explains why it needs to be.

## The 12 weeks

Four runs a week: **reps, a half-marathon-pace tempo, an easy run and a long run.** Half of the running is easy and a quarter is the long run, so even with two quality sessions most of the week stays gentle.

| Week | Phase | Reps | HM-pace tempo | Long run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Base | 5 × 1 km | 15 min | 70 min |
| 2 | Base | 3 × 2 km | 18 min | 75 min |
| 3 | Base | 6 × 1 km | 20 min | 80 min |
| 4 | Base | Light fartlek 8 × 1 min (cutback) | – | 60 min |
| 5 | Build | 6 × 1 km, shorter recoveries | 25 min | 90 min |
| 6 | Build | 3 × 2 km | 30 min | 95 min |
| 7 | Build | 6 × 1 km | 2 × 15 min | 100 min, last 20 at HM pace |
| 8 | Build | Light fartlek 6 × 1 min (cutback) | – | 70 min |
| 9 | Peak | 3 × 2 km | **35 min** | 105 min, last 25 at HM pace |
| 10 | Peak | 6 × 1 km | 30 min | **110 min, last 30 at HM pace** |
| 11 | Peak | Sharpener 4 × 1 km | 15 min | 55 min |
| 12 | Race week | Sharpener 3 × 1 km | – | **Race: 21.1 km** |

The rep recoveries shrink from two minutes to 90 seconds over the weeks, and the tempo grows from 15 minutes to a single 35-minute block.

### The two sessions that matter most

**Week 9: 35 minutes at half-marathon pace.** That's about 6 km at 5:41, a third of the race, as a single block in training. If it goes well, you're ready. If it doesn't, you've learned that with three weeks to spare.

**Week 10: 110 minutes with the last 30 at pace.** The long run peaks here. Eighty easy minutes, then half an hour at 5:41 on tired legs, which is exactly what the last 5 km of the race will feel like.

### Two cutback weeks and a short taper

Weeks 4 and 8 drop the volume and swap both quality sessions for one light fartlek. Don't fill them back up. Then the taper takes 12 days, at roughly 40% and then 60% less volume, keeping short pieces of pace in so your legs remember what 5:41 feels like.

## Race day: 1:59:59 is the door

Even pacing wins. Run the first 5 km at 5:45 rather than 5:30: banking time early costs you more than it saves. Here are the splits for an even 1:59:30, which leaves 30 seconds for the crowded start and the inevitable weave round the drinks tables:

| Point | Time |
|---|---|
| 5 km | 28:19 |
| 10 km | 56:38 |
| 15 km | 1:24:57 |
| 20 km | 1:53:16 |
| 21.1 km | 1:59:30 |

That's 5:40 per kilometre. Your watch will measure the course long by 100–300 m because nobody runs the racing line, so trust the kilometre markers, not your watch's total.

## When to start for a spring half

Count back 12 weeks from race day; the plan starts on the Monday.

| Race day | Plan starts |
|---|---|
| Sun 7 March 2027 | Mon 14 December 2026 |
| Sun 14 March 2027 | Mon 21 December 2026 |
| Sun 21 March 2027 | Mon 28 December 2026 |
| Sun 28 March 2027 | Mon 4 January 2027 |
| Sun 11 April 2027 | Mon 18 January 2027 |

Starting over Christmas is fine. Weeks 1–3 are base weeks, and if a run slips, it's an easy one to move. The [holiday training post]({% post_url 2026-12-18-marathon-training-over-the-holidays %}) next week covers how.
