The hardest part of an interval workout isn't the running. It's the bookkeeping: how long is left, what comes next, when do I switch. Every glance at a screen breaks your rhythm — and at full speed, reading a display is genuinely hard.

That's why iRunning is built to be *heard*, not watched.

## What you hear on a run

With voice cues on, a workout narrates itself:

- **At every interval change**, a clear announcement: *"Run — 2 minutes"*, *"Recover — 90 seconds."* You know what to do and for how long, without looking.
- **In the last three seconds** of an interval, countdown beeps — so the switch never ambushes you. You get those three seconds to brace for the next rep or to savor the end of this one.
- **On the Watch**, a haptic tap lands with the announcement, so even with no headphones you feel the change.

The result: your eyes stay on the trail, your head stays in the effort, and the app quietly handles the arithmetic.

## Your music keeps playing

Here's the detail that matters most in practice: iRunning doesn't stop, pause, or interrupt your audio.

When a cue plays, your music or podcast **ducks** — it briefly gets quieter, the announcement speaks over it, and the volume comes right back. No stutter, no lost place in your audiobook, no silence between songs. It works with whatever you're listening to, because the app isn't a music player fighting for control — it's a voice layered politely on top.

If you prefer silence, that works too: turn announcements off and keep just the beeps, or run fully quiet with haptics only.

## Locked phone, screen off — cues keep coming

Announcements work with the screen off and the phone locked, buried in a running belt or strapped to your arm. The workout keeps narrating from start to finish. Combined with the Live Activity on your lock screen — current interval and time remaining visible at a glance — you can go a whole session without ever unlocking the phone.

## Why audio beats staring at a watch

There's a good body of coaching wisdom behind hearing your workout instead of reading it:

1. **Form.** Looking down at a wrist or a phone changes your posture, especially when you're tired.
2. **Pacing.** Countdown beeps let you finish an interval strong instead of easing off early "because it's probably almost done."
3. **Focus.** Interval training is mentally hard. Offloading the timekeeping frees you to think about effort and form — or to think about nothing at all, which is half the reason we run.

Put your headphones in, press start, and let the app do the counting.
