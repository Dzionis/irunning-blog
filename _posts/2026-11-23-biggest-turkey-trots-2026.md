On Thursday morning more Americans will pin on a bib than on any other day of the year. Last year over 1.3 million people registered for a Thanksgiving race on RunSignup alone, and most of them ran something small: the average Turkey Trot has about 1,200 people. But a handful are enormous, with more runners than most marathons.

Here are the biggest Turkey Trots of 2026, what makes each one different, and which ones you can still enter this week. If it's your first race, our [Turkey Trot 5K guide]({% post_url 2026-11-10-turkey-trot-5k-first-one %}) covers race morning and pacing.

## Which one is really the biggest?

Two races claim the title of America's largest Thanksgiving race, and both have a case.

**Sacramento's Run to Feed the Hungry** is the biggest by recent headcount, at around 28,000 people. It's the 33rd edition this year, and for the first time every runner gets a chip time, not just the competitive wave. It raises money for the Sacramento Food Bank, so for many families it's a Thanksgiving tradition first and a race second.

**The Silicon Valley Turkey Trot** in San Jose has billed itself as the largest for years and drew around 23,000 at its peak. Downtown start, 10K and 5K, and a costume contest before the gun.

Between them, **Austin** and **Dallas** are both over 24,000. Austin's ThunderCloud Subs Turkey Trot starts at the Long Center by Lady Bird Lake and raises money for Caritas of Austin. Dallas has the most unusual main distance of the big ones: **8 miles**, with a 5K for everyone who'd rather be home before the parade.

## The oldest: Buffalo, 131st edition

The **YMCA Buffalo Niagara Turkey Trot** first ran in 1896 with six runners, four of whom finished. It's the oldest continuously run footrace in North America, a year older than the Boston Marathon. The distance is **8K**, the field is capped at about 14,000, and it often sells out in the last week. This year's course is new: a single loop starting and finishing at the Buffalo Niagara Convention Center.

A few more with a long history:

- **Run for the Diamonds**, Berwick, Pennsylvania: 9 miles of hills, run since 1908. The winners get diamonds.
- **Troy Turkey Trot**, New York: 10K and 5K, first run in 1916.
- **Western & Southern Thanksgiving Day Race**, Cincinnati: 117th edition this year, on new downtown courses starting and finishing at TQL Stadium.

## The fast one: Manchester Road Race, 90th running

Most Turkey Trots are a party. The **Manchester Road Race** in Connecticut is also a proper race. It's a loop of **4.737 miles** by the race's own measurement (you'll often see 4.748 or "4.75"), starting and finishing on Main Street at 10:00. There's a real elite field: this year it includes **Sally Kipyego**, the 2012 Olympic 10,000 m silver medallist for Kenya, who later ran the Olympic marathon for the US in Tokyo.

For its 90th running, Manchester gives every finisher a medal for the first time. Behind the elites, around 15,000 people run it in every kind of costume, and the long climb up Highland Street in the first half of the course is the bit everyone talks about afterwards.

**Entry closes online on Tuesday 24 November at 23:59**, and there's no race-day registration. If you want in, do it today or tomorrow.

## The half marathon: Atlanta

Almost every Thanksgiving race is 10K or shorter. The **Atlanta Thanksgiving Day Half Marathon**, run by the Atlanta Track Club, is the biggest exception: a full half on Thursday morning, with a 5K and a mile alongside, starting and finishing at Center Parc Stadium. As of mid-November the mile was full and only a few half and 5K places were left.

A half on Thanksgiving is a different thing from a 5K. It's a real long run, the roads are hilly, and it's late November. If it's your first half, run it as a long run, not a race. ## The parade route: Detroit

The **Detroit Turkey Trot** is the one with the best view: the 10K, 5K and one-mile races run the route of America's Thanksgiving Parade before the parade starts, so runners go down Woodward Avenue between the stands and the balloons. Online registration was due to close on Monday 23 November, so check the race site for any remaining in-person options.

## Watching or tracking someone

Most of the big trots use a timing company with live results online: Race Roster and RunSignup races put results on the event page, usually within a few minutes of each finish. If you're tracking a family member, look up their bib number the night before, and pick a spot near the last half-mile rather than the start. The start is a wall of people, and nobody can find anyone there.
