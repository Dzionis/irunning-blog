Ask a coach what most runners get wrong and the answer is nearly always the same: **the easy runs are too fast.** Not the intervals, not the long run, the boring runs in between. They drift into a medium effort that feels productive and leaves you a little too tired for the sessions that matter.

So how slow is easy? Here's a chart by 5K time, and three ways to check it on the run without doing maths.

## Easy pace by 5K time

A good rule of thumb: **easy pace is about 25–35% slower than your 5K race pace.** The slower end of the range is the right one for recovery days, after a hard session or a race, and in hot weather.

| 5K time | 5K pace (per km) | Easy pace per km | Easy pace per mile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18:00 | 3:36 | 4:30–4:52 | 7:15–7:49 |
| 20:00 | 4:00 | 5:00–5:24 | 8:03–8:41 |
| 22:00 | 4:24 | 5:30–5:56 | 8:51–9:34 |
| 25:00 | 5:00 | 6:15–6:45 | 10:04–10:52 |
| 28:00 | 5:36 | 7:00–7:34 | 11:16–12:10 |
| 30:00 | 6:00 | 7:30–8:06 | 12:04–13:02 |
| 33:00 | 6:36 | 8:15–8:55 | 13:17–14:20 |
| 35:00 | 7:00 | 8:45–9:27 | 14:05–15:12 |
| 40:00 | 8:00 | 10:00–10:48 | 16:06–17:23 |

Two things surprise people about this table.

**It's slow.** A 25-minute 5K runner's easy pace is over a minute per kilometre slower than their race pace. That's normal. Most good training systems put easy running in the same place; the exact percentages vary a little from coach to coach, but not much.

**At the slower end it's close to a walk.** If your 5K is 35–40 minutes, a truly easy run might be 9–10 minutes per kilometre, and a brisk walk is about the same speed. That's fine. Run-walk is a perfectly good way to do an easy run: 3 minutes of slow running, 1 minute of walking. Your heart and legs don't care how it looks.

Don't have a 5K time? Run 5K as hard as you can on a flat route, or use a recent parkrun. Our [guide to good 5K times]({% post_url 2026-10-22-what-is-a-good-5k-time %}) shows where you sit by age.

## Three ways to check it without a chart

Pace depends on the day: wind, hills, heat, sleep and the session before all change it. These three checks work better than any number.

### 1. The talk test

On an easy run you should be able to **speak in full sentences** without gasping between them. If you can only manage a few words at a time, you're running at tempo effort. This is the most reliable check there is, and it's free. If you run alone, try saying your address out loud.

### 2. Heart rate zone 2

Easy running is **zone 2**: 60–70% of your maximum heart rate. If you don't know your maximum, the usual estimates are 220 − age for men and 206 − 0.88 × age for women (the second one fits women's data better than the old formula).

| Age | Zone 2, men | Zone 2, women |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 117–137 bpm | 110–129 bpm |
| 35 | 111–130 bpm | 105–123 bpm |
| 45 | 105–123 bpm | 100–116 bpm |
| 55 | 99–116 bpm | 95–110 bpm |

Age formulas can be off by 10 beats or more for any one person. If the talk test and the numbers disagree, trust the talk test, and look up your real maximum from a hard race or hill session.

If zone 2 forces you to walk on every hill, that's common in the first few months. We wrote a whole post on [why your heart rate is high when you run slowly]({% post_url 2026-09-25-why-heart-rate-high-running-slow %}), and the short version is that it improves faster than you'd think. The [zone 2 guide]({% post_url 2026-09-06-zone-2-running-aerobic-base %}) has the longer version.

### 3. The next day

The simplest test of all: **the day after an easy run, you should feel as if you hadn't run.** If your legs are heavy on the morning of your interval session, your easy runs were too hard. Slow down by 15–30 seconds per kilometre for a week and see what happens to the hard days.

## Why slow works

It feels like slow running can't make you faster. It does, for three reasons:

- **Most of your fitness comes from volume.** Easy running is what lets you run more without getting hurt or exhausted: more capillaries, more mitochondria, stronger tendons.
- **It saves the hard days.** Interval and tempo sessions only work if you arrive fresh enough to hit the paces. Easy days are what make that possible.
- **Medium effort is the worst of both.** It's too hard to recover from quickly and too easy to make you faster. Coaches call it the grey zone.

That's why most training plans, including every plan in iRunning, keep around 80% of running easy and 20% hard.

## Easy days in a time-goal plan

If you're following a plan towards a time (sub-30 5K, sub-60 10K, sub-2 half, sub-4 marathon), only the hard sessions have a pace. The easy runs are run by heart rate, so a tired week or a hot day doesn't push you into the grey zone. For reference, here's where each goal sits:

| Goal | Goal pace | Easy pace from the chart* |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-30 5K | 6:00 /km | 7:30–8:06 /km |
| Sub-25 5K | 5:00 /km | 6:15–6:45 /km |
| Sub-60 10K | 6:00 /km | ~7:00–7:35 /km |
| Sub-2 half marathon | 5:41 /km | ~6:30–7:00 /km |
| Sub-4 marathon | 5:41 /km | ~6:30–7:00 /km |

*For the longer goals, the easy pace comes from the 5K time that usually goes with them (about 28 minutes for sub-60, about 26 for sub-2 and sub-4).

Yes, a sub-4 marathoner's easy runs are around a minute per kilometre slower than marathon pace. That's the plan working, not the runner slacking. Our [sub-4 plan]({% post_url 2026-11-12-sub-4-marathon-training-plan %}) explains how the long runs and pace work fit around it.
