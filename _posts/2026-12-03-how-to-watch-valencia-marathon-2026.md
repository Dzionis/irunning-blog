On Sunday morning around 35,000 people start the **Valencia Marathon**, and the organisers have said out loud what they're hoping for: a **women's world record**. Whether you're following a friend in wave 7 or getting up early to watch the elite race, here's how to do it, from the sofa or from the side of the road.

## Start times

The race starts in **nine waves, ten minutes apart**, from 08:15 Spanish time. The elite runners start at the front of wave 1. Your runner's wave is on their bib.

| Wave | Expected finish | Spain (CET) | UK | New York | Los Angeles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 + elites | under 2:50 | **08:15** | 07:15 | 02:15 | 23:15 Sat |
| 2 | 2:50–2:59 | 08:25 | 07:25 | 02:25 | 23:25 Sat |
| 3 | 3:00–3:11 | 08:35 | 07:35 | 02:35 | 23:35 Sat |
| 4 | 3:12–3:19 | 08:45 | 07:45 | 02:45 | 23:45 Sat |
| 5 | 3:20–3:31 | 08:55 | 07:55 | 02:55 | 23:55 Sat |
| 6 | 3:32–3:42 | 09:05 | 08:05 | 03:05 | 00:05 |
| 7 | 3:43–3:55 | 09:15 | 08:15 | 03:15 | 00:15 |
| 8 | 3:56–4:09 | 09:25 | 08:25 | 03:25 | 00:25 |
| 9 | over 4:10 | 09:35 | 08:35 | 03:35 | 00:35 |

The leaders should finish a little after **10:20** Spanish time (09:20 UK). The course closes at **15:15**: the time limit is 5 hours 30 minutes from crossing the start line, with a cut-off at km 25 at 12:57.

Some schedule sites list a 05:00 GMT start. That's wrong: it's 07:15 GMT.

## How to watch on TV or online

Valencia hadn't published its 2026 broadcaster list when we wrote this. Last year:

- **The race website** streamed the elite race live in Spanish and English, from 08:00 Spanish time.
- **In Spain**, it was on Teledeporte, #Vamos, Eurosport and À Punt, the Valencian public channel.
- **In the US**, ESPN+ carried it.
- **Elsewhere**, the organisers published a per-country list on their "where to watch" page.

We couldn't find a confirmed UK broadcaster for 2025, so the official web stream is the safest bet in Britain. Check the race's "where to watch" page in the last days before the race for 2026.

## How to track a runner

Download the free **Maratón Valencia** app (iOS and Android), open live tracking and search by name or bib number. It shows split times and pace as your runner crosses each timing mat, about every 5 km. Positions during the race are unofficial; final results go up on the race results site.

Two tips:

- **Note the gun time and the wave.** A runner in wave 7 starts an hour after the elites. If the app shows nothing at 08:45, that's why.
- **Watch the split, not the dot.** Use the last 5 km split to estimate when they'll reach you: pace × distance from the last mat.

## Where to watch

Valencia is a great race to watch in person. The course stays inside the city, several streets are used twice, and the **Turia gardens**, a 9-kilometre park in the old riverbed, run through the middle of it. You can walk between most of the good spots in the park without crossing the course.

| Spot | Km (approx.) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Avenida de Balears, by the start | 0–1 | See everyone set off; very busy |
| Avenida Blasco Ibáñez | 8 and 16 | The same corner gives two sightings |
| Passeig de l'Alameda | ~10–15 and again before 31 | Big crowds, beside the Turia gardens |
| Avenida del Port | 23–24 | Wide and lively, close to the halfway mark |
| Turia gardens / Passeig de l'Albareda | 24.5–29 | Easy to reach on foot from the Alameda |
| Ciutat Vella (old town) | 29–31 | Narrow streets, loud crowds |
| Passeig de la Ciutadella to the City of Arts and Sciences | 38.5–41.8 | The last few kilometres to the blue walkway finish |

**Where runners need you most:** the long, quiet stretch past the Bioparc and along **Avenida Tres Creus**, around km 34–38. Few spectators, and it's exactly where the marathon starts to hurt. If you want to make a difference to someone's race, that's where.

### Getting around

- **Use the metro and tram.** Roads near the course are closed and buses are diverted. In recent years Metrovalencia opened at about 06:00 with extra trains connecting to **tram line 10**, which stops at **Amado Granell–Montolivet** right by the start and finish.
- **Walk through the park.** The Alameda and Albareda both run along the Turia gardens, so you can get from around km 10 to km 25–29 and on to the finish at the City of Arts and Sciences on foot.
- **Meet at the finish, but not at the line.** The finish area is crowded and fenced. Agree on a meeting point a few hundred metres away before the race.

## The elite race: a women's world record attempt

The women's world record is **2:09:56**, set by Ruth Chepngetich in Chicago in 2024. Valencia's elite coordinator has said plainly that the record is the goal this year, and the race offers a **€1 million bonus** for it (and the same for a men's record under 1:59:30).

**Women to watch:**

- **Fotyen Tesfay** (Ethiopia) ran **2:10:51** on her debut in Barcelona this March, a minute off the record. She's the fastest woman in the field.
- **Agnes Ngetich** (Kenya), the 2026 world cross-country and world half marathon champion, makes her **marathon debut**. Her half marathon best is 1:03:04.
- **Joyciline Jepkosgei** (Kenya) is the defending champion and course record holder: 2:14:00 last year.
- **Emily Sisson** (USA, 2:18:29) leads the non-African entries.

**The men's race** lost its biggest name on 13 November, when **Yomif Kejelcha**, who ran 1:59:41 in London this year, withdrew injured. That leaves defending champion **John Korir** (Kenya, 2:02:24 here last year, with a negative split) as the fastest man in the field, with Alexander Mutiso (2:03:11), Dawit Wolde (2:03:48) and Nibret Melak (2:04:00). The course record, 2:01:48 by Sisay Lemma, is worth €30,000.

Pacing targets hadn't been announced when we checked. If the women's pacers are told to go through halfway in about **65 minutes**, the record attempt is on.

## The numbers from last year

- **30,523 finishers**, the first time Valencia passed 30,000, and **nine national records**.
- **John Korir** won the men's race in 2:02:24 (1:01:47 and 1:00:37). **Joyciline Jepkosgei** won the women's in 2:14:00, a course record and the fastest time in the world in 2025.
- Britain's **Alex Yee**, the Olympic triathlon champion, ran 2:06:38 on his marathon debut, the second-fastest British marathon ever.

## Weather

Early December in Valencia is usually 12–17 °C during the day and cooler at the start, with a flat course near sea level. The forecast is the thing to watch: wind along the port and the beach strip in the first few kilometres is the main risk.
