Every marathon finish is a good marathon time. That's not a platitude: well under 1% of people ever run one, and the hardest part is the months of training that get you to the start. But "good" also has a more useful meaning, the one you're probably searching for: how does my time compare with everyone else's, and what should I aim for next?

Here are the real numbers. Where finishers actually land, what "good" means for your age and sex, how rare sub-3, sub-4 and sub-5 really are, and how to pick your next target without guessing.

## The short version

| | Women | Men | Everyone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Average US marathon finish** (2024) | 4:51 | 4:24 | 4:34 |
| **Middle of the field** in Chicago 2026 (median) | 4:42 | 4:04 | 4:22 |
| **A first marathon**, typical range | 4:45–5:30 | 4:15–5:00 | |
| **Boston qualifier**, age 18–34 | 3:25 | 2:55 | |
| **World record** | 2:09:56 | 1:59:30 | |

The averages are from RunRepeat's analysis of more than ten million US marathon results; the medians from the 54,581 finishers of the 2026 Chicago Marathon. Big-city marathons attract slightly faster fields than the national average, which is why Chicago's middle runner is quicker.

The men's world record is new: Sabastian Sawe ran **1:59:30** in London in April 2026, the first official marathon under two hours. Ruth Chepngetich's 2:09:56 from Chicago 2024 still stands for women.

In a big-city field:

- **Sub-3 is rare: about 5–8% of finishers.** In Chicago 2026 it was 6.1%, and fewer than 1 in 10 of those were women. It usually takes years of consistent training, not one good block.
- **Sub-3:30 is roughly the top fifth.**
- **Sub-4 is somewhere between a third and a half of the field**: 38% in Chicago, about 39% in London 2024, nearly half in Berlin, which is flat, cool and fast. It's the most popular time goal in running for a reason.
- **About 70% of finishers are under five hours**, and around 1 in 10 take six hours or more.

So if you've run 4:45, you're in the middle of almost any big marathon. If you've run 3:45, you were faster than about three quarters of the people around you at the start.

## By age and sex

Running Level publishes marathon standards by age and sex, built from more than 10,000 race results logged by its users. Its levels are percentiles: **novice** is faster than 20% of runners, **intermediate** faster than half, **advanced** faster than 80%, and **elite** faster than 95%.

One caveat. The people who log their races on a running website are keener than the average marathon field, so these tables are a little faster than real race results. The intermediate man aged 30 runs 3:43, while the average US male finish is 4:24. Treat "intermediate" here as "a solid club-runner time", not as "average".

**Women**

| Age | Novice | Intermediate | Advanced | Elite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 5:22 | **4:36** | 3:58 | 3:28 |
| 30 | 5:03 | **4:19** | 3:44 | 3:16 |
| 40 | 5:15 | **4:30** | 3:53 | 3:24 |
| 50 | 5:46 | **4:56** | 4:15 | 3:44 |
| 60 | 6:23 | **5:28** | 4:43 | 4:08 |

**Men**

| Age | Novice | Intermediate | Advanced | Elite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 4:23 | **3:43** | 3:12 | 2:48 |
| 30 | 4:23 | **3:43** | 3:12 | 2:48 |
| 40 | 4:31 | **3:50** | 3:18 | 2:53 |
| 50 | 4:57 | **4:12** | 3:37 | 3:10 |
| 60 | 5:30 | **4:40** | 4:00 | 3:31 |

Two patterns stand out:

- **There's no penalty for being 40.** Times barely move until the early 40s. In actual race results the fastest age groups are often 35–44: in Chicago 2026 the 35–39 group had the quickest median of all, 4:15, ahead of runners in their twenties. Marathon running rewards years of training more than youth.
- **After about 45, the drop speeds up**: roughly 10–15 minutes every five years at the intermediate level. A 4:30 at 60 is a stronger run than a 4:00 at 30.

## Age grading: one number for any age

To compare runs across ages and sexes, runners use **age grading**: your time as a percentage of the world-best time for your age and sex. The formula is simple, the standard time divided by your time, and the standards are published by World Masters Athletics.

| Age grade | What it means |
|---|---|
| 60%+ | Local class: a good club runner |
| 70%+ | Regional class: wins age-group prizes at local races |
| 80%+ | National class |
| 90%+ | World class |
| 100% | About world-record level for your age |

Most regular marathoners land between 45% and 60%. It's a useful number if you want to know whether your 4:10 at 52 was better than your 3:55 at 38. Often, it was.

## What about your first marathon?

First marathons are slower than people expect, and that's normal. In a study of 138 first-time London marathoners (average age 37), men averaged about 4.5 hours and women about 5.4 hours. Coaches commonly see second and third marathons come in 20–30 minutes faster than the first, mostly from better pacing and fuelling rather than more fitness.

So for a first one, the best time goal is often no time goal: finish, enjoy the last 5 km, and use the result to set a real target next time. Our [first-marathon plan guide]({% post_url 2026-09-22-first-marathon-training-plan-16-weeks %}) is built around exactly that.

## Boston: the "good time" with a deadline

For many runners, a good marathon time means one that qualifies for Boston. The 2027 standards (for the race on 19 April 2027) are:

| Age | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| 18–34 | 2:55 | 3:25 |
| 35–39 | 3:00 | 3:30 |
| 40–44 | 3:05 | 3:35 |
| 45–49 | 3:15 | 3:45 |
| 50–54 | 3:20 | 3:50 |
| 55–59 | 3:30 | 4:00 |
| 60–64 | 3:50 | 4:20 |
| 65–69 | 4:05 | 4:35 |
| 70+ | 4:20 and up | 4:50 and up |

Hitting the standard isn't the same as getting in. For 2027, qualifiers needed to be **5 minutes 17 seconds faster** than their standard to be accepted, and more than 8,000 qualifiers missed out. If Boston is your goal, aim for at least 6 minutes under your standard. Courses with a big net downhill now also get a time adjustment.

## How to pick your next target

1. **Start from a recent race, not a wish.** A half marathon from the last few months is the best predictor. Double it and add 5–15 minutes: a 1:55 half points to about 4:00–4:05, a 2:10 half to about 4:30–4:35. The longer the gap between your half and your marathon, the more the marathon has to cover.
2. **Pick a round number just ahead of you.** If you ran 4:12, sub-4 is a real goal. If you ran 4:45, sub-4:30 is; sub-4 is a two-cycle goal.
3. **Translate it into a pace you can practise.**

| Goal | Per km | Per mile | Halfway |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3:00 | 4:16 | 6:52 | 1:30 |
| 3:30 | 4:59 | 8:01 | 1:45 |
| 4:00 | 5:41 | 9:09 | 2:00 |
| 4:30 | 6:24 | 10:18 | 2:15 |
| 5:00 | 7:07 | 11:27 | 2:30 |
| 5:30 | 7:49 | 12:35 | 2:45 |
| 6:00 | 8:32 | 13:44 | 3:00 |

The halfway column assumes even splits. In practice, a slightly slower first half almost always produces a faster finish; the [negative-split guide]({% post_url 2026-09-08-negative-split-marathon-pacing %}) explains why.
