A screenshot of a map says *I ran*. A video of the route drawing itself across the city, with the kilometres ticking up, says *here's how it went*.

iRunning can now make that video for you. Pick a run, pick a style, tap export, and a few seconds later you have a vertical clip ready for Instagram Stories or TikTok. It's **free for everyone**.

## What's in the video

- **Your route draws itself** behind a moving dot, from the start line to the finish.
- **Four numbers count up as the runner moves:** distance, elapsed time, current pace and heart rate. Pace uses the last 30 seconds of running, so it moves the way your pace actually moved: fast on the reps, slower on the recoveries.
- **The camera follows you** by default, zoomed in, then pulls back at the end to reveal the whole loop.
- **The run's name** (and, in most styles, the date) sits on the card, along with a small iRunning mark.

It's a silent H.264 MP4 at 1080 pixels wide and 30 frames per second, so you can add your own music in Instagram or TikTok.

## Five looks

Swipe the style name above the preview to switch between five designs: **Ink**, **Frosted glass**, **Brand block**, **Side rail** and **Bleach wash**.

## How to make one

1. Open **History** and tap a run with a GPS route.
2. Tap **Share**, then choose **Video** ("Animated route replay").
3. Set it up with the buttons down the left side of the preview:
   - **Format:** 9:16 for Stories, Reels and TikTok, or 2:3 for a feed post.
   - **Map:** light, dark or satellite.
   - **3D buildings:** tilts the camera so the city rises around your route.
   - **Heart rate** and **Time:** show or hide either number.
   - **Whole route:** show the full loop from the start instead of drawing it as you go.
   - **Follow runner**, **zoom** (2.5× to 8×) and **rotate map with heading**.
4. Drag the **Duration** slider anywhere from 5 to 30 seconds. The default of 12 seconds suits a story.
5. Tap **Export video**. A 12-second clip renders in well under a minute.

## Where it goes

When the video is ready you get four options:

- **Instagram** opens Stories with your video already set as the background. Add stickers, music or a caption, then post.
- **TikTok** saves the clip to Photos and opens TikTok, so you can pick it from your camera roll.
- **Save** puts it straight into Photos.
- **Share** opens the usual share sheet for Messages, WhatsApp, AirDrop and so on.

The Instagram and TikTok buttons only appear when those apps are installed.

## Tips for a better clip

- **Sessions with pace changes make the best videos.** Watching the pace jump on every rep, or drop on every float, is far more interesting than a steady line. The loop above has five kilometre surges in it, and you can see each one.
- **Try 3D in cities, satellite on trails.** Buildings give 3D its drama; satellite imagery shows the forest, coast or mountain you ran through.
- **Keep it short.** 10 to 15 seconds holds attention in Stories. Save 30 seconds for the long run you're proud of.
- **Use Whole route for a loop.** A route that returns to the start looks great when the full shape is visible from the first frame.

## What you need

- **A run with GPS.** Treadmill and indoor runs have no route, so there's nothing to animate. For those, share one of the image cards instead.
- **iRunning 1.2.2 or later.** Video export is free: no subscription needed.

Want a still image instead? The same five styles are available as **map photo cards** in **Share › Image**. And if you'd rather put your stats on your own photo, see [how to add transparent run overlays to your Instagram story]({% post_url 2026-08-27-transparent-run-stats-overlay-instagram %}).
