Most marathons worry about the cold. Singapore's start is at **04:30**, in the dark, because by 9 a.m. it's 30 °C. This weekend the race formerly known as the Standard Chartered Singapore Marathon runs for the 44th time, under a new name, a new organiser and, for the first time, a cap on numbers.

Here's what's new and what to expect, whether you're running it or following from another time zone.

Every race starts at the **F1 Pit Building** at Marina Bay and finishes **inside the National Stadium**. The Friday night times come from race guides; the official race page lists Friday as "evening". The expo is at the National Stadium from 3 to 6 December and is free.

## What's new in 2026

**A new name.** Standard Chartered was the title sponsor for over two decades. From this year the race is the **BYD Singapore International Marathon presented by adidas**, and Standard Chartered stays on as the 10K's name. The race also has a new organiser, replacing Ironman Asia after about ten years.

**A cap on numbers.** Last year more than 55,000 people ran across the weekend, and congestion on the course drew complaints. For 2026 the total is capped at **52,000**, with **12,000 marathon places** (9,000 for Singapore residents and 3,000 international). The marathon and the half are both sold out.

**A new course.** Three changes runners will notice:

- **The West Coast Highway climb moved from about km 32 to about km 8,** so it's run early, in the dark and the coolest part of the morning, instead of late in the race in the heat.
- **The course is 99% one-way.** The only out-and-back is about 300 metres on the Marina Barrage.
- **East Coast Park's cycling path** is used for the first time.

**Early trains.** For the marathon on Sunday, selected MRT stretches run from **02:30**, every 15 minutes, as a pilot.

## The course

Point to point: from the F1 Pit Building west past Tanjong Pagar Railway Station to Keppel Bay, back through the city past the Singapore Art Museum and Marina Bay, over the Marina Barrage, east along **East Coast Park**, and back over the **Benjamin Sheares Bridge** to finish in the National Stadium at Kallang.

Total climb is about **96 metres**. The highest point is the Benjamin Sheares Bridge at about **km 37**: not a big hill, but a real one at the worst possible moment.

## The heat

This is what makes Singapore hard. December's average overnight low is about **24 °C**, and at dawn the humidity is close to **90%**. December is also monsoon season, so a tropical shower on race morning is possible.

If you're running:

- **Forget your goal pace.** In heat and humidity like this, a realistic target is 5–10% slower than you'd run in cool weather. That's about 12–24 minutes over a four-hour marathon.
- **Drink early, and take salt.** You'll sweat more than you think in the dark, when it doesn't feel hot.
- **Use the dark.** The first two hours are the coolest of the race. Run them comfortably, and let the sun come up while you still have something left.
- **Pace by heart rate.** In humid heat, heart rate rises at the same pace. Our post on [why heart rate climbs when you run slowly]({% post_url 2026-09-25-why-heart-rate-high-running-slow %}) explains how heat changes it.

## The elite race

Singapore is a **World Athletics Gold Label** race, the only one in Southeast Asia, with US$50,000 for each winner. No 2026 elite names had been announced when we checked.

The course records are modest by big-city standards because of the conditions: **2:11:25** for men (Luke Kibet, 2009, run in 31 °C) and **2:28:54** for women (Priscah Cherono, 2019).

Last year Uganda's **Abel Boniface Sikowo** won the men's race in 2:15:40, his second win in a row (he was promoted to the 2024 title after the first finisher was disqualified for doping), and **Shuko Genemo** of Ethiopia won the women's in 2:41:24. **Soh Rui Yong** won the Singapore national title in 2:46:23, the first runner to win the national half and marathon titles on the same weekend.

## A little history

The race started on 5 December 1982. In 2019 the marathon moved to an evening start, part of Singapore's bid at the time to become a World Marathon Major. It caused hours of traffic jams, and the race went back to the pre-dawn start. This weekend it's Shanghai that's being assessed for the Majors. Our [Shanghai preview]({% post_url 2026-12-02-shanghai-marathon-2026-world-major-assessment %}) has that story.

## How to follow it

Last year the race was streamed live on YouTube, with live results on the race website. The 2026 livestream and tracking details hadn't been announced when we checked; look on the official site during race week.

In Europe, the marathon starts at **20:30 on Saturday evening** UK time. That's the same evening Shanghai starts, at 23:00, and Valencia goes off at 07:15 on Sunday morning. It's a long night for marathon fans.
