Few marathons have grown as fast as **Abu Dhabi**. The first edition in 2018 had about 10,000 people across all its races. Last year it had more than **37,000**. This year, its eighth, it moves back to the waterfront **Corniche** and runs for the first time as a **World Athletics Gold Label** race.

It's on **Saturday 12 December**, and it's still open. Here's what to know.

## Back to the Corniche

Last year the race started and finished at Zayed Sports City for one year. In 2026 it returns to the **Corniche**, the long seafront boulevard of palms, beaches and towers on the north-west side of the city. The detailed 2026 route map hadn't been published when we checked. Recent editions passed landmarks such as Qasr Al Hosn, the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, ADNOC's headquarters and Capital Gate.

The course is **flat**, on wide roads, which is why Abu Dhabi has produced fast times from the start.

## The weather: cool, then not

December is the best month to run in Abu Dhabi. Overnight lows are around **15–19 °C**, and it's still cool when the gun goes off in the dark. But the sun comes up at about 07:00, and daytime highs reach **25–26 °C**. Humidity at dawn can be very high, sometimes over 90%.

So: the first hour or two is near-perfect marathon weather. For anyone running four hours or more, the second half is warm and sunny with little shade. Start conservatively, drink at every station, and expect to slow down a little after sunrise. That's the heat, not you.

## From 10,000 to 37,000

| Year | Participants |
|---|---|
| 2018 | about 10,000 |
| 2023 | 25,000+ |
| **2025** | **37,000+**: about 5,000 marathon and relay, 8,000 10K, 11,000 5K and 13,000 2.5K |

That last line is the interesting one: most people in Abu Dhabi run the short races. It's a city festival with a marathon attached, not the other way round. A Marathon Village opens on the Corniche from 7 December, the week before.

In April 2026 World Athletics upgraded the race to **Gold Label**, the second-highest level for road races, which means stricter standards for the elite field, course and organisation.

## The elite race

The 2026 elite field hadn't been announced when we checked. Winners received US$50,000 last year, from a total pool of over US$300,000.

**2025 results:**

- **Men:** Kaan Kigen Özbilen (Turkey) won in **2:07:27**, ahead of Asrar Hiyrden Abderehman (2:08:09) and Kenneth Kiprop Kipkemoi (2:08:32).
- **Women:** Catherine Amanang'ole (Kenya) won in **2:21:17**, the first woman to defend the Abu Dhabi title, ahead of Edna Kiplagat (2:25:07) and Burtukan Bekele (2:25:25).

The **course records** both have a story. Reuben Kipyego set the men's 2:04:40 in 2019 as a pacemaker who decided to keep going, and won. Brigid Kosgei's 2:19:15 in 2023 took almost a minute and a half off the women's record. The fastest times from the first edition in 2018 don't count: that course was later found to be about 200 metres short, probably because of a misplaced turnaround cone at km 34.

## How to follow it

In recent years the race was streamed live on the official website and YouTube, with Abu Dhabi Sports Channel as a media partner. The 2026 stream and tracking details hadn't been confirmed when we checked. With a pre-dawn start in Abu Dhabi, Europe watches in the middle of the night and the US on Friday evening.
