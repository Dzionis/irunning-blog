If you're training for a spring marathon, Christmas lands at an awkward spot: far enough into the plan that you've built some momentum, far enough from race day that a missed week doesn't feel like it matters. Add travel, family, late nights and a lot of food, and the holidays are where a lot of spring plans quietly fall apart.

They don't have to. You don't need a perfect week, you need a good-enough one. Here's how to get through the next two weeks with your plan intact.

## Where your plan is at Christmas

For a 16-week marathon plan, here's where the big spring races put you during the week of 21–27 December:

| Race | Race day | Plan started | Christmas week is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo | Sun 7 Mar | Mon 16 Nov | Week 6 |
| Barcelona | Sun 14 Mar | Mon 23 Nov | Week 5 |
| Boston | Mon 19 Apr | Mon 28 Dec | Week 1 starts the Monday after |
| London | Sat 24 / Sun 25 Apr | Mon 4 Jan | Two weeks before week 1 |

So if you're running Tokyo or Barcelona, you're in the base-to-build part of the plan: no race-pace work yet, long runs around 1:45 to 2:00. That's good news. The sessions this early are flexible, and nothing you do in one week makes or breaks the race.

If you're running Boston or London, the holidays are the run-up. Use them to get consistent, not to get fit: three or four easy runs a week, so week 1 doesn't feel like a shock. The full list of spring races with their plan-start Mondays is in our [spring 2027 calendar]({% post_url 2026-11-07-spring-2027-marathons-when-to-start-training %}).

## The rule: protect two runs, flex the rest

Every week of a marathon plan has a few runs that matter more than the others. Over the holidays, protect these two:

1. **The long run.** It's the most important session of the week at every stage of marathon training. Do it on whichever day is quietest: Christmas Eve morning, Boxing Day, or the Sunday after. Before everyone else is up is usually the easiest time.
2. **One quality session.** Tempo, hills or intervals, whatever your plan has this week. If you can only fit one, keep this one and make it a bit shorter.

Everything else, the easy runs, can move, shrink or disappear for a week. Missing two easy runs out of four in one week makes no measurable difference to a marathon in March. Missing four long runs in a row does.

## A minimum week for travel and family days

If the plan says five runs and you can manage three, here's what to keep:

| Normal week | Holiday week |
|---|---|
| Easy run | **Keep, shortened to 30 min** |
| Quality session | **Keep** |
| Easy run | Drop |
| Easy run | Drop, or a 20-minute shake-out |
| Long run | **Keep, full length** |

That's about 60–70% of the usual volume, which is roughly what a cutback week looks like anyway. If your plan has a cutback week near Christmas, swap it so it lands on the busiest week. Your legs won't know the difference.

## When moving isn't enough: pausing

Sometimes the holidays mean a real break: a week skiing, a long-haul trip, a family thing that takes over everything. Then pausing the plan is better than ignoring it, because a plan you've ignored for ten days is a plan you'll be tempted to abandon.

**Pause for one week if you'll miss most of it.** You come back to the same week you left. On a 16-week plan with a spring race, one week is usually absorbable: you lose one of the base weeks, and the taper still lands where it should. Check how many weeks you have left before the race. If the plan's last week would fall after race day, skip a cutback week later rather than cutting the taper.

**Don't pause for two weeks or more** unless you're ill or injured. After two weeks off, restart the last week you completed rather than the next one.

**Be honest about illness.** A cold above the neck, an easy run is fine. Anything in the chest, a fever, or aching muscles: don't run. Pause, recover, and restart the week.

## Food, drink and late nights

You don't need a holiday diet. Three things are worth knowing:

- **Long run after a big dinner?** Most people run fine the morning after a big meal. A heavy dinner the night before is basically carb loading. Drink water before bed if there was wine.
- **Alcohol hits sleep, and sleep is where training sticks.** It's not the drink, it's the 5 hours of bad sleep after it. Put the long run on the morning after the quiet night.
- **Don't run off the food.** An extra hour of running to "earn" Christmas dinner is how people end up injured in January. The plan already has the right amount of running in it.

## Cold, dark and away from home

- **Running somewhere new?** A loop of 3–5 km you can repeat is easier than an out-and-back you don't know. Ask whoever lives there.
- **Treadmill at the hotel or a relative's gym?** It's a perfectly good place for a quality session. Our [treadmill guide]({% post_url 2026-09-10-treadmill-intervals-indoor-running %}) has the details.
- **Running in the dark?** A headlamp and something reflective. More in our [winter running guide]({% post_url 2026-11-05-winter-running-guide %}).

## Doing it in iRunning

If you follow your plan in iRunning, the holidays are a few taps:

- **Move a session.** Long-press any session in the plan and choose *Do it today* or *Move to* the next free day. Sessions move within the week, so the plan keeps its shape.
- **Skip an easy run.** Long-press, *Skip this session*. No guilt banner, and the plan carries on.
- **Pause the plan.** Open the plan, tap ⋯ › *Manage plan* › *Pause plan*, and pick one week, two weeks or *Until I resume*. The weeks shift forward instead of counting down, and nothing is marked as missed while it's paused.
- **Restart the week.** Back from a break and not sure where you are? *Restart week* lets you repeat the whole week or just redo the sessions you missed. Everything you've logged stays.
- **Ran without the plan?** If you went for a run with another app or your watch's Workout app, it comes in from Apple Health and counts towards the plan session for that day.

## The short version

Long run: keep. One quality session: keep. Easy runs: whatever fits. A week off for a good reason: pause, don't quit. And enjoy the holidays: a marathon plan is 16 weeks long because it can absorb a few imperfect ones.

*Plan-start dates count 16 weeks back from race day. Training advice, not medical advice.*
