A calendar of the 2027 World Marathon Majors has been going round running feeds this month: eight races, eight dates, a registration status and an elevation figure for each. It's a useful picture, and most of it holds up. But a few things on it have already changed, and the elevation numbers depend a lot on who measured them.

So we checked every line against the official race websites on 12 November 2026. Here's the whole 2027 series in one place: the dates, where entry stands today, how hilly each course really is, and the Monday a 16-week plan would start for each race.

## What's changed since the charts went round

**Berlin has a new name.** BMW is no longer the title sponsor: from 2027 the race is the **Generali Berlin Marathon**, with a new website at generali-berlin-marathon.com. If you're searching for 2027 entry details, look under the new name.

**Berlin's lottery has closed.** It ran from 1 October to 12 November 2026. Everyone who entered gets an email with the result on **Thursday 3 December**, and the €215 fee is charged from 4 December only if you're drawn. Fast runners with a certified time from 2025 or 2026 apply outside the lottery, and Jubilee Club members (ten or more Berlin finishes) have guaranteed entry.

**Cape Town is a Major now.** The Sanlam Cape Town Marathon passed its second assessment and joins as the eighth Major. Sunday 23 May 2027 is its first edition with the status.

**There's no Eight Star medal.** The Six Star medal for finishing the original six stays as it is, and runners who've done all eight don't get a new medal. The next one planned is a **Nine Star** medal, and it only happens if Shanghai becomes the ninth Major. Its second and final assessment is the Shanghai Marathon on **Sunday 6 December 2026**. If it passes, it joins in 2027.

**London is two days, split by sex for the fast fields.** About 100,000 people run the same Greenwich-to-The-Mall course across Saturday 24 and Sunday 25 April. The elite, Championship and Good For Age men run on the Saturday, the women on the Sunday, and the mass start is mixed on both days. You run on one day only.

**New York's course changed.** In 2026 the route changed for the first time since 2011: NYRR straightened the Bronx section, so older elevation figures for New York describe a course that no longer exists.

## Where entry stands

### Waiting for results

- **Berlin**: results by email on **Thursday 3 December**.
- **Chicago**: drawing results on **Tuesday 8 December**. If you're drawn, you pay by 15 December. Charity places stay open until 10 September 2027 (minimum $2,500 fundraising), and tour-operator applications until 10 August 2027. The entry fee is $250 for US residents and $260 for everyone else.

### Coming up

- **New York City**: NYRR hasn't announced the 2027 drawing yet. In 2026 it ran from 4 to 25 February, with the draw on 4 March. Time qualifiers for 2027 use races run in calendar year 2026, so a fast marathon or half before 31 December still counts. Our [New York 2027 guide]({% post_url 2026-10-31-how-to-get-into-nyc-marathon-2027 %}) covers all the routes in.

### Already decided for 2027

- **Tokyo**: the general lottery results came out on 18 September and payment closed on 30 September. The fee is ¥19,800 for residents of Japan and $230 for everyone else. The 2028 lottery should open around mid-August 2027, going by previous years.
- **Boston**: there's no lottery. 33,649 qualifiers applied, and 25,630 got in: 24,630 who beat the cut-off of **5 minutes 17 seconds** under their standard, plus 1,000 drawn at random from qualifiers who missed it. Charity applications open later this autumn. The qualifying window for 2028 has been open since 19 September 2026, with the same standards as 2027 (2:55 for men and 3:25 for women aged 18–34).
- **London**: a record 1,338,544 people entered the ballot, and results went out in July. Good For Age, for UK residents only, closed on 29 October. Charity places are still available.
- **Cape Town**: the lottery ran in June. Charity entries are open, and so are tour-operator packages.
- **Sydney**: the ballot closed on 19 October and results arrived on 3 November. If you missed out, the Charity Program and Travel Program are the remaining ways in.

Every door into each race, with qualifying times, is in our earlier piece on [how to get into a Major in 2027]({% post_url 2026-10-16-how-to-get-into-marathon-major-2027 %}).

## How hilly is each course?

Here's the number everybody wants, and the one nobody agrees on.

| Race | Total climb (FindMyMarathon) | What the organiser says | Where it climbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tokyo** | 60 m | No figures published | Almost nowhere |
| **Berlin** | 73 m | No figures published | Nowhere to speak of |
| **Chicago** | 74 m | No figures published | Roosevelt Road, just before the finish |
| **London** | 127 m | 246 ft (75 m) | Small rises, nothing long |
| **Cape Town** | 205 m | No figures published | Rolling |
| **Boston** | 248 m | Net drop of 459 ft (140 m) | The Newton hills, miles 16–21, ending with Heartbreak Hill |
| **Sydney** | 262 m (2025 course) | +313 m, net −83 m (2026 course) | The Harbour Bridge early, then rolling |
| **New York City** | 294 m (2026 course) | Not readable on nyrr.org when we checked | Five bridges, Fifth Avenue, Central Park |

The calendar going round uses older figures from the same site: 247 m for New York (from before the Bronx change), 273 m for Sydney (an earlier course) and 128 m for London. The organisers' own numbers differ again: London says 75 m, Sydney 313 m.

Three things make the numbers move:

- **GPS watches add climb that isn't there.** A watch's altitude wobbles by a metre or two all the time, and over 42 km those wobbles add up to tens of metres of "climbing" on a flat road.
- **Bridges confuse the maps.** Elevation models read the ground or the water under a bridge, not the road deck. So the Verrazzano, the Queensboro or the Sydney Harbour Bridge can vanish from a profile, or turn into a dip. Our own [New York course guide]({% post_url 2026-10-18-nyc-marathon-course-guide %}) measured about 100 m from the course line. FindMyMarathon, which corrects for bridges, gets 294 m.
- **Courses change.** New York in 2026 and Sydney between 2025 and 2026 are both different from the courses the older figures describe.

So compare the courses with each other, not the numbers between sites. On any one method the order barely changes. **Tokyo, Berlin and Chicago are the flat three**, London is close behind, and **Boston, Sydney and New York** are the ones where the course decides your pacing.

Boston is the odd one out. It drops 140 m from Hopkinton to Copley Square, so it's net downhill and still one of the hardest. The downhill comes first and wrecks your quads. Then the Newton hills come in miles 16–21, and Heartbreak Hill, the last of them, climbs about 26 m (85 ft) exactly where a marathon starts to hurt.

## When does the plan start?

Marathon plans in iRunning (Marathon and Sub-4 Marathon) are 16 weeks long and end on race day, so for a Sunday race week 1 starts on the Monday 111 days earlier:

| Race | 2027 race day | 16-week plan starts |
|---|---|---|
| Tokyo | Sun 7 Mar | **Mon 16 Nov 2026 (today)** |
| Boston | Mon 19 Apr | Mon 28 Dec 2026 |
| London | Sun 25 Apr | Mon 4 Jan 2027 |
| Cape Town | Sun 23 May | Mon 1 Feb 2027 |
| Sydney | Sun 29 Aug | Mon 10 May 2027 |
| Berlin | Sun 26 Sep | Mon 7 Jun 2027 |
| Chicago | Sun 10 Oct | Mon 21 Jun 2027 |
| New York City | Sun 7 Nov | Mon 19 Jul 2027 |

Boston is a Monday race, so its plan ends on the Sunday before. If your London place is for the Saturday, your race comes a day before the plan's last session, so move that session to the Saturday. Nothing else changes.

If you're running Tokyo, today is week 1. The [Sub-4 guide]({% post_url 2026-11-12-sub-4-marathon-training-plan %}) has the paces if four hours is the goal. For everyone else waiting on Berlin or Chicago: the results come out in early December, and the plan doesn't start until June either way. There's plenty of time to decide what to do with the answer.

On a hilly course, train on hills. That's not more hill sessions. It's long runs on a route that climbs where your race climbs: late for Boston and New York, early for Sydney. In the app you pick a start date and see your finish date, so you can check any race that isn't in the table.
