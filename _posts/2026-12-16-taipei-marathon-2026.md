The last big marathon of 2026 starts in the shadow of **Taipei 101**. On **Sunday 20 December**, about 28,000 people run the Taipei Marathon and half marathon, a World Athletics Gold Label race that has quietly become one of the best places in Asia to run a fast time: more than **400 runners have broken three hours** there in each of the past two years.

Here's the guide, for runners and for anyone following along.

Race guides give a time limit of 5 hours 30 minutes for the marathon and 3 hours for the half; check your race pack. On Saturday there's a **Christmas Breakfast Run** of 3K and 9K.

## The course

The marathon is a tour of the city's landmarks, then a long stretch along the river:

- **The start** at City Hall Plaza in Xinyi, with Taipei 101 overhead.
- **Downtown**: past **Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall** and **Taipei Main Station**, then north along Zhongshan Road past the **Taipei Fine Arts Museum**.
- **Over the river** to Dazhi, crossing the Zhongshan and MacArthur bridges.
- **An elevated expressway**: roughly km 18–26 runs on the raised Huandong Expressway. It's exposed, so if there's wind, this is where you'll feel it. Find a group.
- **Riverside parks** along the Keelung River, roughly km 28–36: flat, quiet and a good place to settle into a rhythm, or to start suffering.
- **The finish** on the track inside **Taipei Municipal Stadium**, which makes for a good last 300 metres.

It's mostly flat, with short ramps onto bridges and the expressway. The official elevation figures weren't published when we checked.

## Weather

December in Taipei is cool and humid. Expect around **12–16 °C at the start** and humidity of about 80%. The northeast monsoon can bring drizzle. For most runners that's close to ideal, though the humidity makes it feel warmer than it reads once the sun is up. Dress for the finish temperature, not the start.

## Why so many sub-3s?

Taipei became a World Athletics **Gold Label** race in 2024, and since then it has had **400+ sub-3-hour finishers** each year. The flat course, the cool weather and an early start help, and the organisers lean into it: there are sub-3 pace groups and a SUB-3 badge for those who make it. There's also a separate guaranteed-entry group for fast runners alongside the lottery.

For Taiwanese runners there's a NT$330,000 bonus for breaking the national record, for both the top man and the top woman.

## The elite race

Taipei usually announces its international elite field late: last year's 17 elites were named two days before the race. Last year's results set a high bar:

- **Men:** Kenya's **Bethwel Kibet** won in **2:09:31**, 13 seconds off the course record, ahead of Jonathan Samanayo Korir (2:09:51) and Wilfred Kirwa Kigen (2:10:30).
- **Women:** Ethiopia's **Gadise Mulu** set a course record of **2:24:17**, with Tirfi Tsegaye (2:24:49) also under the old record of 2:25:55.
- The first Taiwanese man was **Shih Yu-tso** in 2:22:59, on his marathon debut.

The men's course record of 2:09:18 is from 2020. With a good day and a deep field, it's within reach.

## Race weekend

- **Expo and bib pick-up**: at the Taipei Expo Dome, 17–19 December. International runners collect in person with their passport.
- **Getting there**: the start is in Xinyi, close to the MRT. Plan to arrive early: 06:30 comes round fast in December darkness.

## How to watch

Last year the race was streamed **free worldwide on the MOMO SPORTS YouTube channel**, with coverage from about 06:00 Taipei time. The 2026 stream hadn't been confirmed when we checked, but the same channel is the place to look.

From Europe that's late on **Saturday evening**: the marathon starts at 22:30 UK time. In the US, it's Saturday afternoon or early evening.

## The end of the season

Taipei is the last big marathon of 2026. After this, it's Christmas Day parkrun and the New Year's Eve 10Ks. ## Running Taipei with iRunning

- **A pace band for sub-3.** Build your race with a 4:10–4:16 per km band, and you'll hear a cue when you drift outside it. On the expressway, where wind can push you around, that's useful.
- **Heart rate for the humidity.** A heart-rate cap keeps you honest once the sun comes up.
- **Apple Watch.** Run with the watch only and leave the phone in your bag.
- **Share the finish.** A share card or a route video from Taipei 101 to the stadium is a nice way to end the year.
