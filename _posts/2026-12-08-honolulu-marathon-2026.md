The **Honolulu Marathon** breaks most of the rules of big-city marathons. There's no qualifying time, no ballot, no entry limit, and **no time limit**: the finish stays open until the last person crosses it. It starts at **5 a.m.**, in the dark, and it's one of the biggest marathons in the US. Last year a record **42,962 people** entered the race weekend.

On **Sunday 13 December** it's the 54th edition. Here's what to know.

## No cut-off, really

"There is no cut-off time!" is on the race's FAQ page, exclamation mark included. Every runner who starts and keeps moving gets an official time and a medal, whether that takes three hours or eleven. That's why Honolulu is so popular with first-time marathoners and walkers, and why about **28% of entrants come from Japan**, where it has been a bucket-list race for decades.

It's also one of the oldest big marathons in the US: first run in 1973, and in 1995 billed as the largest marathon in the world, with 34,434 entrants.

## The course

A point-to-point loop of the south shore of Oahu, starting downtown and finishing in Waikiki:

| Miles | What you'll see |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Downtown and Chinatown: Aloha Tower, Iolani Palace, Honolulu Hale (and, traditionally, fireworks at the start) |
| 4–5 | Ala Moana, then **Kalakaua Avenue through Waikiki**, past the Duke Kahanamoku statue |
| 6–9 | Kapiolani Park and the **first climb round Diamond Head** |
| 10–14 | Kahala, then out along the Kalanianaole Highway |
| 15–19 | The Hawaii Kai loop, by Koko Head and Maunalua Bay |
| 20–24 | Back along the highway and Kahala Avenue |
| 24–26.2 | **Diamond Head again**, past the lighthouse, and down into Kapiolani Park to the finish |

The course is near sea level at both ends, but it isn't flat. The climb round **Diamond Head** comes twice: around miles 7–9 and again around miles 24–25. The highway section in between rolls. Estimates of the total climb range from about 450 to 640 feet. The organisers just say "short, uphill grades".

## The heat

This is what makes Honolulu slow. It's in the **mid-60s °F (about 18 °C) at the 05:00 start** and in the **low 80s (about 28 °C) by 10 a.m.**, with high humidity. Last year it rained, too.

- **The first two hours are your best running.** It's dark and relatively cool. Don't waste them by going too fast; save something for after sunrise.
- **Set a heat-adjusted goal.** Most runners finish 5–15% slower than they would in cool weather. For a first marathon, forget the clock entirely.
- **Drink at every station and take salt.** You'll sweat a lot more than in a cold December race.
- **Walk the Diamond Head hill on the way back if you need to.** Plenty of people do, and there's no cut-off to worry about.

## Aid stations and gels

- **16 aid stations**, the first at about mile 2.5, then every 2–3 miles, with water, sports drink and medical help.
- **Gels** (Precision Fuel PF 30) at about **miles 9, 12, 15 and 20**.
- There's **no bag check at the start**. Drop your bag at Kapiolani Park on Saturday between 10:00 and 17:00 in the official clear bag.
- **Free shuttles** run from the Honolulu Zoo to the start between 02:00 and 04:00.

Runners line up by bib colour: white for elites, green for 2:30–3:59, blue for 4:00–4:59, orange for 5:00–5:59 and purple for 6 hours and over.

## Expo and bib pick-up

At the **Hawaii Convention Center**: Thursday 10 December 10:00–18:00, Friday 11 December 09:00–19:00 and Saturday 12 December 09:00–17:00. Pick-up is in person only: **no proxy pick-up**. Bring the confirmation card emailed to you in race week. There's no parking on Thursday or Friday.

The finisher shirt is handed out in Kapiolani Park after the finish and isn't mailed, so don't leave without it.

## The elite race

The 2026 marathon elite field hadn't been announced when we checked. Last year:

- **Tsegay Weldlibanos** (Eritrea), the CIM course record holder, won the men's race in **2:13:38**.
- **Calli Hauger-Thackery** won the women's race in **2:30:43**, the first British woman ever to win Honolulu.

Each winner gets $25,000 and a **solid gold medal**, and there's a $15,000 bonus for a race record. The course records, 2:07:59 and 2:22:15, have stood since 2019 and 2017.

On Saturday, the **Kalakaua Merrie Mile** down Waikiki's main street has a strong field this year, with Americans Nico Young and Ethan Strand and Britain's Oliver Hoare.

## How to watch and track

- **Track runners** on the official site or app with **Track My Run**, by bib number.
- **Last year's TV** was on Hawaii News Now (K5) from 06:00 to 09:00 Hawaii time, also streamed on its YouTube channel. The 2026 broadcast hadn't been announced when we checked.
- **At the finish**, a big screen in Kapiolani Park shows the finish line on a delay of about 40 minutes.
- **Best spots:** Kalakaua Avenue in Waikiki (mile 5), Diamond Head Road and Kapiolani Park, where you can see runners on the way out (miles 6–9) and on the way back (miles 24–26), and the finish.

From the mainland, the start is at **10:00 Eastern** and **07:00 Pacific**. From Europe it's mid-afternoon: 15:00 in the UK.
