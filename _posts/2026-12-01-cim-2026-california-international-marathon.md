If American runners had to pick one marathon to chase a fast time, most would pick CIM. The **California International Marathon** runs point to point from Folsom to the State Capitol in Sacramento, drops 366 feet on the way, and usually gets cool, still December weather. Close to a third of its finishers qualify for Boston.

On **Sunday 6 December** it's also the **USATF Marathon Championships** for the second year running, so the front of the race will be as interesting as the middle. Here's everything about this year's race, for runners and for anyone watching.

## The course: downhill, but not flat

CIM's reputation is "net downhill", and that's true: you finish 366 feet lower than you start. It's also a bit misleading. The organisers describe it as **rolling hills for the first 21 miles, then flat and fast for the last five**, and those rollers are what decide most people's race.

| Miles | What to expect |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Downhill from the start. Easy to go too fast |
| 1–6 | Gentle rollers through Orangevale |
| **6.5–9** | **The hardest section:** a run of short, steady ups and downs |
| 10–11 | The biggest single climb, around mile 10.5 (about 40 ft), then a drop into Fair Oaks |
| 13.1 | Halfway at Fair Oaks Blvd & Delaware Ave; the relay changes at 13.4 |
| 14–21 | Rolling through Carmichael and along Fair Oaks Blvd to Watt Ave |
| 21.5 | The H Street Bridge, a short rise over the American River |
| 22–26.2 | Flat through Sacramento State, East Sacramento and Midtown, finishing on Capitol Mall |

GPS measurements put the total climb at somewhere between 450 and 660 feet, depending on whose watch you believe. Either way, it's not a lot for a marathon, but you'll feel it if you race the uphills in the first ten miles.

### How to pace the rollers

**Run by effort, not by pace, until mile 21.** Let your pace slow by a few seconds a mile on each rise and come back on the way down. If you try to hold an exact pace up every hill, you spend energy you'll want in Midtown.

**Don't bank time on the first mile.** It's downhill and you'll feel great. Run it at goal pace, not faster.

**The race starts at the H Street Bridge.** The last five miles are flat, and that's where CIM pays you back for a patient first 20. Our [negative split guide]({% post_url 2026-09-08-negative-split-marathon-pacing %}) explains why.

## Aid stations

There are **17 aid stations**, at miles 2.2, 4.2, 6.4, 8.5, 10.1, 12.0, 13.7, 15.3, 16.7, 18.0, 19.4, 20.3, 21.4, 22.3, 23.5, 24.5 and 25.3.

- **Every station** has water and Precision Hydration carb and electrolyte drink (30 g carbs and 500 mg sodium per 16 oz).
- **Three stations** also have Precision Fuel gels (including caffeinated), chews and bananas. Check the official aid-station page for which three, and carry your own gels if you rely on a specific one.
- **Tables are on both sides** for the first three stations, then on the **right side only**. Stay right from mile 4.
- **New in 2026:** three self-refill water stations at about miles 6.7, 14 and 20.4.

## Pacers

The SRA Pace Team runs even-pace groups **every five minutes from 2:55 to 4:05**, plus 4:20, 4:30, 4:35, 4:50, 5:05, 5:20 and 5:50. The start is self-seeded in areas by expected finish time: a seeded start under 2:45, then 2:45–3:00, 3:01–3:15, 3:16–3:30, 3:31–4:00 and 4:00+. Start entrances close at **06:50**.

## Boston qualifying

CIM is USATF-certified, and its drop is within the limit Boston allows, so there's no adjustment to your time. The organisers say more than **32%** of finishers qualify for Boston, and about 2,300 runners qualified last year. For Boston 2028 the standards start at 2:55 for men and 3:25 for women aged 18–34, but you'll need to beat your standard by a margin: in 2027 the cut-off was 5 minutes 17 seconds.

A few BQ goals as even splits per mile:

| Goal | Per mile | Per km | Half split |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2:55:00 | 6:41 | 4:09 | 1:27:30 |
| 3:05:00 | 7:03 | 4:23 | 1:32:30 |
| 3:25:00 | 7:49 | 4:52 | 1:42:30 |
| 3:35:00 | 8:12 | 5:06 | 1:47:30 |

On CIM, a first half a minute or so slower than this and a faster second half is a perfectly good way to hit the number.

## The championship race

CIM is the **2026 USATF Marathon Championships** for American men and women, with $20,000 to each national champion and prize money down to 10th place. There's also a bonus for anyone who runs under the Olympic Trials qualifying time (2:16 for men, 2:37 for women), which has turned CIM into the place to chase a trials standard. The 2026 elite field hadn't been published when we checked.

Last year's championship was one of the best in years:

- **Men:** Futsum Zienasellassie won in 2:09:29, with Joe Whelan (2:09:41) and Christian Allen (2:09:57) behind. All three broke the American course record.
- **Women:** Molly Born won on her marathon debut in **2:24:09**, a course record, ahead of Sara Hall (2:24:36, a masters course record) and Megan Sailor (2:25:16).
- **106 runners** went under the Olympic Trials standard: 52 men and 54 women, from a single race.

The women's course record has fallen three years in a row. With a championship field and decent weather, it could go again.

## Weather

Early December in Sacramento is usually cool and dry: the low to mid-40s °F (about 5–8 °C) at the start and the mid-50s (about 13 °C) by the time most people finish. Dress for the finish, with a throwaway layer for the wait at the start.

## Race weekend logistics

- **Expo and bib pick-up:** SAFE Credit Union Convention Center, Hall D. Friday 4 December 13:00–19:00 and Saturday 5 December 09:00–17:00. **No race-day pick-up.** Bring photo ID and your QR code.
- **Getting to the start:** official buses from Sacramento are included in your entry. Private drop-offs at the start aren't allowed.
- **Gear check:** at the expo or at the bus loading area; there's none at the start.

## How to watch and track

- **Track runners** with the **RTRT.me** app.
- **The championship race** streams live on **USATF.tv** from 06:55 Pacific (09:55 Eastern, 14:55 in the UK).
- **The mass finish** streams on YouTube from about 09:45 to 13:30 Pacific.
- **Best spots:** the start is closed to spectators. Good cheer zones are Fair Oaks Blvd (mile 10), halfway at Delaware Ave, Watt Ave (19.4), the H Street Bridge (21.5), Sacramento State (22) and around Sutter's Fort (24.5). Stay on the **north side of the course** to cross fewer roads. The finish on Capitol Mall has bleachers and a big screen.

## Racing with iRunning

- **A pace band for the flat finish.** Build your race as a workout with a pace range on the last five miles, and you'll hear *"Speed up"* if you fade after the bridge.
- **Heart rate for the rollers.** A heart-rate cap on the first 20 miles keeps you honest on the hills.
- **Apple Watch, phone-free.** Run CIM with just the watch on your wrist and the phone in your gear bag.
- **Share the finish.** A share card or a route video from Folsom to the Capitol is a nice thing to have from a PB.

Good luck on Sunday. Stay right after mile 4, and save it for the bridge.
